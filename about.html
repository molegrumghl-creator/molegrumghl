The writer

A short, honest introduction.

My name is Ronald Aduda, and I write Molegru'm GHL, a plain-language gut health site. It exists because most bloating advice is either vague or overwhelming: the kind that tells you to cut out everything, and the kind that tells you nothing at all.

What I share here is general education, written in plain language. It is not a substitute for advice from a clinician who knows you and your history.

If a post here helps you have a more useful conversation with a clinician, it has done its job. If it replaces that conversation, that's a problem.

What this site covers

Everyday foods, everyday digestion, plain language.

The posts here focus on the foods people actually eat most often — bread, rice, dairy, onions, beans, oats, coffee, fizzy drinks — and the small, practical things that make bloating better or worse. Mealtimes, portions, chewing, stress at the table, tight waistbands, and the food diary you keep for two weeks and never look at again.

There is no supplement shelf here, no list of "superfoods", and no promise of a fix. What there is: a few clear explanations, a structured way to test foods one at a time, and a printable plan for working out your own pattern.

Topics you'll find here

  • Bloating after meals and what usually causes it
  • The common everyday trigger foods, and why they vary from person to person
  • A calm, one-food-at-a-time way to test your own triggers
  • When to stop self-testing and see a clinician instead
  • Ordinary habits that quietly make bloating worse

Who it's for

Written for adults who want something practical.

This site is written for adults who feel bloated after meals most days, have no red-flag symptoms, and would like a calm, structured way to spot their own food patterns — without a paid app, a long elimination diet, or a book of strict rules.

It's also for the reader who has already tried cutting out whole food groups and found the results didn't stick. The approach here is deliberately smaller: one food at a time, written down, then decided.

Not medical advice

Everything on this site is general education, not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. It is not a substitute for seeing a qualified clinician about your own symptoms.

If you have blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, severe or worsening pain, persistent vomiting, a fever alongside digestive symptoms, new digestive changes after the age of 50, or a family history of bowel conditions, please speak to a doctor rather than self-testing with food.
