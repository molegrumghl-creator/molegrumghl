What it is

A short, structured plan — not a list of foods to avoid.

Most bloating advice gives you a long list and tells you to cut everything out. That's hard to follow and tells you almost nothing about which of those foods actually bother you.

The Trigger Finder is the opposite. It's a small guide plus a printable tracker that helps you build a short, honest diary, test one suspect food at a time, and end up with a clear answer written in your own handwriting. Two weeks, roughly, at your own pace.

It is written for ordinary kitchens — bread, rice, dairy, onions, beans, oats, coffee, fizzy drinks — not for a specialist diet clinic.

Who it's for

Adults who feel bloated after meals most days, have no red-flag symptoms, and would like a calm, structured way to spot their own pattern — without a paid app, a long elimination diet, or a book of rules.

Not for you if you have blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, severe pain, or any symptom your doctor is already investigating. See a clinician instead.

What's inside

Six plain sections. No filler.

  • Normal vs not-normal bloating A short, honest explanation of what bloating usually is, what causes it, and the symptoms that mean you should be talking to a clinician rather than keeping a food diary.
  • A 3-day baseline diary Three pages to fill in before you change anything. What you ate, when, and how you felt one and two hours afterwards. The aim is to see your normal week clearly — not to change it yet.
  • A one-food-at-a-time testing plan The step-by-step method: pick one suspect, remove it for a few days, reintroduce it, note the result, then move on. Everything else stays the same, so you can actually tell what changed.
  • A results page One clean page to write your final answer: which foods are problems, which are fine, and which you're still unsure about. Something you can bring to a consultation or keep for yourself.
  • A when-to-see-a-doctor page A short list of the symptoms that should stop the self-testing and send you to a qualified clinician — plain language, no scare tactics.
  • Printable, pocket-sized Designed to print at home on ordinary paper. Fits on a fridge, in a notebook, or folded into a bag for the week.

How it works

The plan in five steps.

  1. Three days of notes, nothing changed

    Eat normally. Write down what you ate and how you felt an hour later. This is the reference point everything else is compared against.

  2. Pick one suspect

    From your notes, choose the single food that appeared most often before the worst episodes. One food only.

  3. Remove it for three to five days

    Keep everything else the same — same meals, same portions, same times, same coffee. Otherwise you won't know what changed.

  4. Bring it back

    Have a normal portion and note what happens over the next few hours. A clear yes or no is what you're after.

  5. Write it down, then move on

    Record the result on the results page. Then repeat with the next suspect. A couple of suspects is often enough to start seeing a pattern.

A note on what this is not

The Trigger Finder is not a medical test, a diagnosis, or a treatment. It does not replace advice from a doctor or a registered dietitian. It is general education, written for adults with mild to moderate bloating who want a calm way to spot their own food patterns.
